Title: Biofertilizers
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Biofertilizers are products that contain selected living microorganisms. Rhizobium helps specific legumes nodulate. Azospirillum and Azotobacter are used with some cereals and vegetables. Phosphate-solubilizing bacteria are intended to help plants use phosphorus already in the soil. They are not magic powders. The correct strain, a live packet, moist soil, and a matching crop all matter. Read the label for the crop and the expiry date.

Benefits:
- Supports nitrogen fixation in legumes when the strain matches
- Low application quantity
- Can improve use of existing soil nutrients
- Fits organic systems when the formulation is allowed

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Buy a packet labelled for your crop and check the expiry date and storage instructions.
2. Mix seed treatment with a sticker such as jaggery solution or the method printed on the packet. Keep the seed out of direct sun.
3. Sow into moist soil the same day when the label says to.
4. For legume crops, do not expect nodulation if the seed was treated with a forbidden fungicide or if the soil is extremely dry.

Required materials:
- The correct biofertilizer packet
- Clean water
- A shaded mixing place

Practical examples:
- Rhizobium on groundnut
- Azotobacter on wheat
- Phosphate-solubilizing bacteria with compost in a vegetable bed

Related crops: Groundnut, Wheat, Sugarcane

Precautions:
- A packet left on a hot dashboard is no longer a live inoculant.
- Do not mix with chemical fungicides unless the manufacturer explicitly allows that combination.
- Use the strain specified for the crop. A soybean Rhizobium is not the right choice for every legume.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Companion planting
Category: pest
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Companion planting is a form of intercropping chosen for pest and soil effects as well as yield. Evidence is stronger for some pairs than for others. Marigold with tomato is widely used because the flower can reduce some nematode pressure and it attracts beneficial insects. Basil and onion are traditional companions in kitchen gardens. Companion planting does not make a crop immune. It is one layer beside sanitation, soil health, and monitoring.

Benefits:
- Adds diversity in a small space
- Can support natural enemies with nectar and shelter
- Some combinations suppress specific soil pests
- Gives a second harvest

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Pick a companion that you will actually use or sell.
2. Give both plants enough light and root space. A companion sown too thick becomes a weed.
3. Use marigold as a border or inter-row in solanaceous crops rather than as a complete control method.
4. Observe whether pest damage is lower than in a nearby single-crop bed. Keep the comparison honest.

Required materials:
- Companion seed
- A spacing plan

Practical examples:
- Marigold around tomato
- Onion near carrot in a garden bed
- Coriander left to flower beside vegetables as a wasp nectar source

Related crops: Tomato, Chilli, Cotton

Precautions:
- A companion from the same disease family can make the problem worse.
- Do not rely on companion planting alone for a serious outbreak.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

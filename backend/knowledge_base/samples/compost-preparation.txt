Title: Compost preparation
Category: compost
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Compost is decomposed organic matter. A good pile mixes nitrogen-rich green materials with carbon-rich brown materials, stays about as moist as a wrung sponge, and is turned so air reaches the centre. Heat in an active pile kills many weed seeds and pathogens, but the edges stay cooler, so turning matters. Finished compost is dark, crumbly, and smells like forest soil, not ammonia or rot.

Benefits:
- Recycles on-farm waste.
- Improves soil structure and nutrient holding.
- Reduces the volume of residue and the viability of many weed seeds when the pile heats evenly.
- Gives a stable amendment that is safer for seedlings than fresh manure.

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Choose a shaded, well-drained spot away from wells and kitchen doors.
2. Chop bulky stalks so they wet through. Mix roughly two to three parts dry brown material with one part fresh green material or dung, by volume.
3. Build the pile in layers about one to one and a half metres high. Sprinkle water so every layer is moist.
4. Cover with straw, banana leaves, or a perforated sheet to limit drying and nutrient loss in heavy rain.
5. Turn the pile after it heats, commonly after one to two weeks, and again two or three times. Move the outside material to the middle.
6. Cure the compost until it no longer heats and plant roots would be safe in it. This often takes two to four months depending on the mix and the weather.

Required materials:
- Crop residue and dry leaves
- Fresh weeds that have not set seed
- Dung if available
- Water
- A cover
- A fork for turning

Practical examples:
- Rice straw mixed with cow dung and green weeds, turned three times, is a typical farm compost for the next nursery.
- A pile that smells of ammonia needs more dry leaves and a turning. A pile that is white and dusty needs water.

Related crops: Rice, Wheat, Tomato, Mango

Precautions:
- Do not compost meat, plastic, treated wood, or pesticide-contaminated plants if the compost is for an organic field.
- Keep the pile out of floodwater so nutrients do not wash into a well or stream.
- A pile that never heats may still mature, but it will not kill weed seeds reliably.

Title: Compost
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Finished compost is the default organic fertilizer on this farm guide. Apply it where roots will grow, in amounts the farm can actually produce or buy. Vegetables and orchards usually show the benefit first. Cereals need larger volumes, which is why green manure and rotation sit beside the compost heap in a practical plan.

Benefits:
- Broad soil improvement
- Safer for seed than fresh waste
- Recycles residues
- Supports later use of microbial drenches

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Use only finished compost that is dark, cool, and crumbly.
2. Spread and incorporate it during land preparation.
3. On perennial crops, apply under the canopy and mulch over it.
4. Keep records of how many cartloads went to each field.

Required materials:
- Finished compost

Practical examples:
- Basal dressing for wheat
- Pit mixture for banana

Related crops: Wheat, Banana, Rice, Sugarcane

Precautions:
- Unfinished compost can rob nitrogen and carry weed seeds.
- Compost made from sprayed crop waste can contaminate an organic field.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

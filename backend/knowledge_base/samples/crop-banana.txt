Title: Banana organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Banana is a heavy-feeding perennial herb that needs deep, fertile soil, shelter from strong wind, and frequent water. Organic plantations use large quantities of compost, mulch, and clean planting material.

Suitable soil: Deep loam or alluvial soil with drainage. The plant hates stagnant water around the corm even though it needs frequent irrigation.
Climate: tropical humid
Sowing season: Planting can be staggered through the year where frost is absent. Choose sword suckers or tissue-culture plants that are free of visible weevil and virus symptoms. Avoid the coldest weeks in subtropical areas.
Water need: high

Organic fertilizers: Fill pits with topsoil and compost or farmyard manure before planting. Reapply compost and mulch several times a year because the plant grows fast. Wood ash, if uncontaminated, is sometimes used as a potassium source; do not use ash from treated timber.

Irrigation: Drip or basin irrigation at short intervals. Mulch heavily with dried leaves and pseudostems after harvest. Drainage channels matter as much as the water supply.

Natural pest management: Use clean suckers, pare and inspect the corm, and destroy stems that show rhizome weevil. Remove wilted mats rather than replanting in the same hole immediately. Bag bunches where scarring beetles or fruit scarring is common. Keep the mat to a mother, a daughter, and a follower so air can move.

Harvesting: Harvest the bunch when fingers are full and the angles have rounded, before they split. Cut carefully with a helper supporting the bunch. Dehand and pack without bruising.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

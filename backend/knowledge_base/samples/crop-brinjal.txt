Title: Brinjal organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Brinjal, or eggplant, is a warm-season vegetable that benefits from healthy nursery seedlings, steady moisture, and frequent harvest. Rotate it away from tomato, chilli, and potato to help reduce shared soil-borne problems.

Suitable soil: Fertile, well-drained loam or sandy loam with good organic matter. Avoid fields with standing water or recent solanaceous crops.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: Raise seedlings in a clean nursery and transplant sturdy plants when the weather is warm. The best season varies by region and local pest pressure.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Apply mature compost before transplanting and replenish with compost as the crop grows. Neem cake may be used as a soil amendment where locally appropriate; avoid fresh manure near roots.

Irrigation: Keep moisture even during flowering and fruit development. Use mulch and water at the root zone; avoid waterlogging.

Natural pest management: Scout for shoot and fruit borer, remove infested shoots and damaged fruit promptly, and use healthy seedlings. Rotate with unrelated crops and conserve natural enemies. Apply only locally permitted organic controls according to their labels.

Harvesting: Pick fruits while glossy and firm, before seeds become hard. Use clean shears to avoid damaging branches and harvest regularly to encourage continued fruiting.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Cabbage organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Cabbage is a cool-season leafy vegetable that needs steady moisture and fertile soil while the head forms. Timely planting helps avoid heat-related loose heads and bolting.

Suitable soil: Moisture-retentive but well-drained loam or clay loam with organic matter. Avoid acidic, compacted, or waterlogged beds.
Climate: cool
Sowing season: Raise seedlings in a clean nursery and transplant during the locally recommended cool season. Choose a variety suited to the local temperature and market.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Incorporate mature compost before transplanting. A light compost side-dressing during leaf growth may help; avoid late excess nitrogen that can delay head maturity.

Irrigation: Maintain even moisture from transplanting through head formation. Mulch to reduce moisture swings and ensure excess water drains away.

Natural pest management: Use crop rotation, insect netting on small plots, and regular checks for caterpillars and aphids. Hand-remove pests and damaged leaves where practical, and conserve beneficial insects.

Harvesting: Harvest firm, well-formed heads before they split. Cut with a clean knife and remove crop residues that may carry pests or disease.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Chilli organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Chilli needs a warm season, full sun, and soil that drains after rain. Organic crops are managed with compost, mulch, and close watching for thrips, mites, and fruit rot.

Suitable soil: Sandy loam, loam, or well-managed black soil. Standing water around the stem invites wilt.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: Kharif transplanting follows the early monsoon in many areas. A rabi crop suits milder winters with irrigation. Transplant sturdy seedlings rather than weak, shaded ones.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Basal compost or farmyard manure, neem cake as a soil amendment, and a later vermicompost side-dressing support flowering and fruit set. Excess fresh nitrogen pushes leaves at the expense of fruit.

Irrigation: Light, regular watering is better than long droughts followed by flooding. Mulch reduces soil splash onto fruit. Reduce water as ripe fruit is picked if plants are otherwise healthy.

Natural pest management: Yellow sticky traps help monitor sucking pests. Neem oil or insecticidal soap can suppress aphids, thrips, and mites when coverage reaches the leaf underside. Remove and bury rotten fruit. Avoid planting immediately after another solanaceous crop.

Harvesting: Harvest green or fully coloured fruit with the stalk, using scissors in wet weather so wounds are clean. Dry red chilli on clean raised racks, not on soil.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

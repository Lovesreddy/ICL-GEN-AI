Title: Cotton organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Cotton is a long-season kharif fibre crop. Organic cotton depends on deep soils, early sowing, trap cropping, and biological control because bollworms and sucking pests can otherwise cause heavy loss.

Suitable soil: Deep black cotton soils and well-drained loams. Shallow, crusting soils restrict the taproot.
Climate: semi-arid
Sowing season: Sow with the break of the monsoon or under pre-sowing irrigation so the crop flowers before peak late-season pest pressure. Use delinted, clean seed from a trusted source.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Compost or farmyard manure at land preparation, and a green manure where the rainy season allows, build the slow nutrient supply cotton needs. Biofertilizers such as Azotobacter may be used as seed treatment according to the label.

Irrigation: Moisture is critical at square formation, flowering, and boll development. Avoid prolonged waterlogging. Stop late irrigation that only delays maturity and invites pests.

Natural pest management: Intercrop or border the field with pigeon pea, maize, or marigold as a trap and refuge for natural enemies, matching the local extension recommendation. Scout twice a week. Remove rosette flowers where budworm is active. Release or conserve Trichogramma where that programme is available. Neem-based sprays and, when permitted by the organic standard, Bacillus thuringiensis are tools against caterpillars. Do not spray when bees are foraging.

Harvesting: Pick clean, dry seed cotton from fully opened bolls. Keep plastic, leaves, and stained fibre out of the sack. Store off the ground.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

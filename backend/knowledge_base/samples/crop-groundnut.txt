Title: Groundnut organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Groundnut is a legume grown for its underground pods. Loose sandy loam lets pegs enter the soil. As a legume it fixes nitrogen when nodulated, so organic nutrition focuses on phosphorus, calcium, and organic matter rather than heavy nitrogen.

Suitable soil: Sandy loam or sandy soil that does not crust. Hard clay makes pegging and harvest difficult. Slightly acidic to neutral soils are preferred.
Climate: semi-arid
Sowing season: Kharif sowing with the monsoon is common. Irrigated rabi or summer crops need varieties of a suitable duration. Sow into moist soil at an even depth.
Water need: low

Organic fertilizers: A modest basal dressing of compost is enough on fertile land. Seed treatment with Rhizobium suited to groundnut supports nodulation. Gypsum at pegging is widely used as a calcium source for pod fill; confirm it is accepted by the certification body if the farm is certified.

Irrigation: The crop tolerates short dry spells but needs moisture at flowering and pod fill. Over-irrigation increases leaf diseases. Stop water as pods mature.

Natural pest management: Earthing up supports pegs and can reduce some soil insects. Collect leaf-miner or caterpillar-damaged leaves if the outbreak is local. Neem cake in the furrow is used against some soil pests. Dry the harvest quickly so aflatoxin-forming fungi do not develop on moist pods.

Harvesting: Lift when the inner shell of most pods has darkened and the kernels fill the shell. Dry pods until they rattle and store them dry and ventilated.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

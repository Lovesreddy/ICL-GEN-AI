Title: Lentil organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Lentil is a cool-season pulse crop with modest water needs. As a legume it contributes to crop rotations and can use residual fertility when established in a well-drained seedbed.

Suitable soil: Well-drained loam or sandy loam. Avoid saline, waterlogged, or heavily compacted fields.
Climate: cool dry
Sowing season: Sow in the cool rabi window at the locally recommended time so flowering and pod fill occur before severe heat. Use clean seed of a locally adapted variety.
Water need: low

Organic fertilizers: Use mature compost where soil organic matter is low and avoid heavy nitrogen. A compatible Rhizobium seed inoculant may improve nodulation; follow its label and local advice.

Irrigation: Often grown on residual moisture. If irrigation is available, protect the crop from stress at flowering and pod formation without saturating the soil.

Natural pest management: Rotate with cereals, use clean seed, and scout for aphids and pod borers. Remove volunteer pulse plants and conserve beneficial insects. Avoid repeated pulse crops in the same field.

Harvesting: Harvest when most pods turn brown and plants are dry but before pods shatter. Dry the harvested plants on a clean surface and store seed in dry, pest-proof containers.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

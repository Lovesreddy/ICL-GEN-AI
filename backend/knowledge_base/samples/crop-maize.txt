Title: Maize organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Maize is a warm-season cereal used for grain, food, and fodder. It performs best in fertile, well-drained soil with reliable moisture around tasseling and silking.

Suitable soil: Deep, fertile loam or sandy loam with good drainage. Avoid standing water and compacted layers.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: Kharif sowing begins with dependable monsoon moisture. Irrigated rabi and summer crops are possible where local temperatures and water supply suit the chosen variety.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Incorporate mature compost or well-rotted farmyard manure before sowing. Rotate with a legume or use a green-manure cover crop to support soil nitrogen. Use seed inoculants only when recommended for the variety and region.

Irrigation: Keep the seed zone moist for emergence. Avoid water stress from tasseling through early grain fill, and avoid waterlogging at every stage.

Natural pest management: Use locally adapted seed, rotate with non-cereal crops, and scout whorls for caterpillars. Remove badly infested plants where practical and conserve natural enemies. Use only locally approved organic products and follow their labels.

Harvesting: Harvest grain when husks are dry and kernels are hard. Dry ears thoroughly before shelling and store grain clean and dry.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

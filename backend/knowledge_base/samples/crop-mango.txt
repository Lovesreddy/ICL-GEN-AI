Title: Mango organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Mango is a long-lived fruit tree. Young orchards need watering, mulch, and protection from grazing animals. Organic care concentrates on canopy sanitation, composted manure, and timely attention to hoppers and anthracnose.

Suitable soil: Well-drained red, laterite, or loamy soils. The tree grows in a wide range of soils if water does not stand around the trunk.
Climate: tropical
Sowing season: Plant grafted saplings at the start of the rainy season or whenever irrigation can support the first year. The crop itself is perennial; flowering follows a dry period in many tropical climates.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Apply composted farmyard manure in a ring under the drip line at the start of the monsoon, and keep the trunk contact-free so the bark stays dry. Mulch with dried leaves. A legume cover in the inter-row adds organic matter while trees are young.

Irrigation: Water young trees regularly. Bearing trees often flower better after a distinct dry spell. Irrigate at fruit set and fruit growth if the soil becomes hard and dry. Do not wet the flowers with sprinklers.

Natural pest management: Prune crossing and diseased twigs after harvest. Collect and destroy mummified fruit. Hopper and anthracnose pressure is managed by open canopies, monitoring at flowering, and sprays permitted by the organic standard, such as neem formulations timed away from peak pollinator activity. Follow the product label and the certifier's list.

Harvesting: Pick mature fruit by hand with a small stalk, using poles and nets rather than shaking the tree. Ripen off the tree in a clean, ventilated room.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Okra organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Okra, also called lady's finger, is a warm-season vegetable that grows quickly in full sun. Regular picking and early pest scouting help keep plants productive.

Suitable soil: Well-drained loam or sandy loam enriched with mature organic matter. Avoid cold, waterlogged soil at sowing.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: Sow directly when the soil is warm and frost risk has passed. Stagger sowing where a longer harvest period is desired.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Incorporate mature compost before sowing. A modest compost side-dressing can support flowering; avoid excess nitrogen that produces leafy growth with fewer pods.

Irrigation: Water after sowing and maintain moderate, even moisture during flowering and pod development. Mulch the soil and avoid prolonged waterlogging.

Natural pest management: Inspect young leaves and shoots for sucking pests and borers. Remove affected plant parts, use insect barriers on small plots, and conserve beneficial insects. Rotate with non-malvaceous crops.

Harvesting: Pick tender pods every few days before they become fibrous. Wear gloves if the variety has irritating leaf hairs and keep harvested pods shaded.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

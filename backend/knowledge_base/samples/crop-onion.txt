Title: Onion organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Onion is a shallow-rooted bulb crop that needs a fine seedbed, steady early growth, and good drainage. The best planting season and day-length response depend on the locally adapted variety.

Suitable soil: Loose, fertile loam or sandy loam with good drainage and a fine tilth. Avoid saline or waterlogged beds.
Climate: mild
Sowing season: Use a locally adapted short-, intermediate-, or long-day variety and follow regional nursery and transplanting windows. Transplant sturdy seedlings rather than overgrown ones.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Mix mature compost into the bed before transplanting. Avoid excessive fresh nitrogen late in the season, which can delay bulb maturity and reduce storage quality.

Irrigation: Water gently after transplanting and keep moisture even during leaf growth and bulb formation. Stop watering as tops fall and bulbs mature; ensure drainage after heavy rain.

Natural pest management: Use clean seedlings, rotate away from onion-family crops, and scout for thrips and leaf diseases. Remove diseased residues and avoid overhead irrigation late in the day. Follow local organic rules for any treatment.

Harvesting: Harvest when most tops naturally bend and bulbs have formed dry outer skins. Cure bulbs in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated place before storage.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

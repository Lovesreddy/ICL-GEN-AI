Title: Potato organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Potato is a cool-season tuber crop that needs loose soil, healthy seed tubers, and even moisture. Good drainage and crop rotation are important to limit tuber diseases.

Suitable soil: Loose, friable sandy loam or loam with good drainage and organic matter. Avoid compacted soil and fields with a recent history of potato diseases.
Climate: cool
Sowing season: Plant healthy, locally recommended seed tubers in the cool growing window. Planting dates vary with altitude and region; avoid hot conditions during tuber initiation.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Work mature compost into the bed before planting. A legume rotation can improve soil structure and nutrient cycling. Avoid fresh manure in contact with seed tubers.

Irrigation: Maintain even soil moisture from tuber initiation through bulking, while keeping ridges well drained. Reduce irrigation as foliage naturally matures.

Natural pest management: Use certified or disease-free seed tubers, rotate away from solanaceous crops, and earth up to protect tubers from light. Remove volunteer plants and diseased foliage. Confirm any organic treatment is approved locally.

Harvesting: Harvest after the skin has set and vines have mostly dried. Lift carefully to avoid cuts, cure in a cool shaded place, and store in a dark, ventilated area.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Soybean organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Soybean is a legume grain crop that can fix nitrogen when compatible root bacteria are present. It needs a well-drained seedbed and sufficient moisture during flowering and pod fill.

Suitable soil: Well-drained loam or clay loam with a near-neutral pH. Avoid fields that remain waterlogged after rain.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: Sow with reliable early monsoon moisture using a locally recommended, disease-free variety. Seed treatment with the appropriate Rhizobium inoculant may help where soybean has not been grown recently; follow the product label.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Use soil-test guidance to plan phosphorus and potassium sources. Mature compost supports soil structure; avoid heavy nitrogen inputs that can suppress nodulation.

Irrigation: Rainfed crops need good drainage. Where irrigation is available, prioritize flowering and pod filling, and avoid waterlogging.

Natural pest management: Rotate with cereals, use clean seed, and scout for defoliators and pod borers. Conserve parasitoids and predators; use locally approved biological controls only when monitoring indicates a need.

Harvesting: Harvest when most pods are mature and leaves have yellowed, before pods shatter. Dry seed to a safe storage moisture and keep it in clean, sealed containers.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

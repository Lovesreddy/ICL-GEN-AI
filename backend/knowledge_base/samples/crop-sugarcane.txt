Title: Sugarcane organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Sugarcane is a tall grass grown for its sugary stalks. It occupies the land for a year or more and removes large amounts of nutrients, so organic fields need planned compost, trash mulching, and a rotation or green manure between long ratoons.

Suitable soil: Deep loam, clay loam, or alluvial soil that drains. Shallow soils limit ratoon life.
Climate: tropical
Sowing season: Planting dates vary by region: adsali, eksali, and suru or pre-season systems exist. Use two- or three-bud setts from a healthy, pest-free nursery. Avoid setts from ratoon fields that showed red rot or smut.
Water need: high

Organic fertilizers: Trench compost or farmyard manure before planting, trash mulch after harvest, and biofertilizers such as Azospirillum where recommended. A green-manure crop before planting improves the next cane. Do not expect a small dose of liquid organic preparation to replace tonnes of bulky organic matter on this crop.

Irrigation: Water at regular intervals in the formative months. Trash mulch saves water and suppresses weeds. Drainage is necessary on heavy soils. Withhold water shortly before harvest according to the local milling practice so sucrose concentrates.

Natural pest management: Early shoot borer and top borer are managed by using healthy setts, earthing up, removing dead hearts, and conserving parasitoids. Trichogramma releases are used in some organic programmes. Avoid long, unbroken ratoons. Rogue smut whips and red-rot stools and do not use them as seed.

Harvesting: Cut mature cane close to the ground, strip trash, and send cane for processing quickly because delay lowers recoverable sugar. Keep setts for replanting separate from mill cane.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

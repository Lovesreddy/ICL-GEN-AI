Title: Tomato organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Tomato is a fruit vegetable that needs sun, steady moisture, and fertile but well-drained soil. Organic production emphasises nursery hygiene, compost, staking, and prevention of blight and fruit borer.

Suitable soil: Sandy loam to loam, rich in organic matter, with good drainage. Avoid poorly drained clay that stays wet around the crown.
Climate: warm
Sowing season: In many plains regions a rabi crop is most reliable. Kharif and summer crops need disease-tolerant varieties and reliable drainage or irrigation. Raise seedlings in a sterile or solarised nursery mix.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Mix compost or vermicompost into the bed before transplanting. A handful of neem cake in the planting hole is a traditional soil amendment. Side-dress with compost at flowering. Foliar fermented preparations such as diluted panchagavya are used by some farmers; keep them off open blossoms if they attract insects.

Irrigation: Drip irrigation or furrow watering at the base is preferable to wetting the leaves. Keep moisture even from flowering through fruit swell to reduce blossom-end problems and cracking. Mulch to conserve water.

Natural pest management: Marigold borders and removal of damaged fruit reduce fruit borer. Neem oil helps with aphids and whiteflies. Stake plants for airflow. Remove leaves that stay wet against the soil. For late blight pressure, use resistant varieties, wide spacing, and morning irrigation.

Harvesting: Pick at breaker or full colour depending on market distance. Harvest in the cool part of the day and do not pile soft fruit deeply.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Turmeric organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Turmeric is a rhizome spice crop planted as the monsoon begins. It prefers loose, fertile soil and partial shade in very hot sites. Organic growers treat seed rhizomes carefully and rely on compost and mulch.

Suitable soil: Friable loam, sandy loam, or red soil with organic matter. Heavy waterlogged clay rots the rhizomes.
Climate: warm humid
Sowing season: Plant at the start of kharif, when rains begin and soil is warm. Use healthy fingers with at least one sound bud, from a crop that did not show rhizome rot.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Basal compost or farmyard manure mixed into ridges or beds, plus neem cake, is a standard organic combination. Mulch immediately after planting. A later vermicompost application supports rhizome bulking.

Irrigation: Keep soil moist but not flooded until the monsoon is established. Irrigate during dry breaks. Good drainage is part of disease control.

Natural pest management: Seed selection is the main protection against rhizome rot and scale. Treat seed rhizomes with a permitted biological product such as Trichoderma when the certifier allows it, following the label. Remove plants that yellow in patches and improve drainage. Shoot borer damage is reduced by field sanitation and by cutting and destroying infested shoots if the incidence is low.

Harvesting: The crop is ready about seven to nine months after planting, when leaves yellow and dry. Dig carefully, boil or cure and dry according to the local process, and store dry rhizomes away from damp.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

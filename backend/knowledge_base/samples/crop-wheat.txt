Title: Wheat organic cultivation guide
Category: crop
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Wheat is a rabi cereal suited to cool, dry winters and loamy soils with good drainage. Organic wheat depends on residual fertility from a preceding legume or green manure, plus compost worked in at land preparation.

Suitable soil: Well-drained loam and clay loam. Waterlogged or very sandy soils reduce tillering and grain fill.
Climate: cool dry
Sowing season: Rabi. Sow after the monsoon has receded and the soil has cooled, commonly from late October to November in much of India. Late sowing shortens the grain-filling period.
Water need: medium

Organic fertilizers: Apply decomposed farmyard manure or compost at final tillage. A preceding mung or cowpea crop adds nitrogen. Seed treatment with a crop-appropriate biofertilizer such as Azotobacter is a common organic practice; follow the product label.

Irrigation: The first irrigation supports crown-root initiation. Later irrigations are most useful at tillering, flowering, and milk stage. Avoid waterlogging.

Natural pest management: Rotate away from continuous cereals, remove volunteer plants, and use clean seed. Aphids can be checked with neem oil sprayed in the evening and by conserving ladybird beetles. Termite-prone fields benefit from well-decomposed manure rather than raw straw left against the seed.

Harvesting: Harvest when grain is hard and straw has turned yellow. Dry thoroughly. Store in clean containers and inspect for weevils.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

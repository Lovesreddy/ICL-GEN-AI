Title: Farmyard manure
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Farmyard manure is a mixture of dung, urine-soaked bedding, and leftover fodder that has been heaped until it decomposes. Well-rotted manure is dark and does not smell strongly of ammonia. It is the backbone of fertility on many mixed organic farms. Fresh manure from the shed is not the same product.

Benefits:
- Adds organic matter in bulk
- Supplies a broad range of nutrients slowly
- Improves water holding on sandy soil
- Recycles livestock waste

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Heap manure under shade, keep it moist, and turn it until it is no longer hot or fibrous.
2. Spread it before the final tillage so it sits in the root zone.
3. On trees, spread it under the drip line and keep it off the bark.
4. Follow it with mulch or incorporation the same day in hot sun so nitrogen is not lost from the surface.

Required materials:
- Decomposed farmyard manure
- Transport and a fork
- Mulch or soil cover

Practical examples:
- Basal manure for rice before puddling
- Annual ring application for mango

Related crops: Rice, Wheat, Mango, Sugarcane

Precautions:
- Fresh manure burns seedlings and can introduce weed seeds.
- Do not pile manure where rain washes it straight into a well or pond.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

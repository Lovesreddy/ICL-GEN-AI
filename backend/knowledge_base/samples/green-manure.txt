Title: Green manure
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Green manures such as dhaincha, sunn hemp, and cowpea are grown for six to eight weeks and incorporated before they become woody. Leguminous green manures add nitrogen if they nodulate. They also add fresh organic matter and shade out some weeds. The following crop benefits only if the green manure is turned in with enough time and moisture to decompose.

Benefits:
- Adds nitrogen when a legume nodulates
- Protects bare soil
- Suppresses some weeds
- Improves the next seedbed

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Sow a locally adapted green-manure seed into moist soil after the previous harvest or just before the monsoon.
2. Let it grow until flowering begins. Older, woody plants decompose slowly.
3. Cut and incorporate it into moist soil, commonly two to three weeks before sowing or transplanting the main crop.
4. If the window is short, compost the cut biomass instead of burying a large fresh mass against seed.

Required materials:
- Green-manure seed
- Moisture for decomposition
- A way to cut and incorporate the crop

Practical examples:
- Dhaincha before rice
- Cowpea before wheat where irrigation allows a short summer crop

Related crops: Rice, Wheat, Sugarcane

Precautions:
- A thick layer of fresh green matter can temporarily tie up nitrogen and produce gases in waterlogged soil.
- Buy seed that is not contaminated with parasitic weeds.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

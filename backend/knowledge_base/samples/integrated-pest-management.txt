Title: Integrated pest management
Category: pest
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Integrated pest management, or IPM, is a way of deciding. It fits organic farming when every control you choose is allowed by the organic standard. The sequence is prevention, observation, identification, threshold, and only then a control. Cultural methods come before biological methods, and biological methods come before botanical sprays. Calendar spraying, where a product is applied every week regardless of what is on the plant, is the habit IPM is meant to replace.

Benefits:
- Fewer unnecessary sprays
- Better use of natural enemies
- Decisions you can explain to a certifier
- Records that improve next season

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Prevent problems with variety, rotation, healthy seedlings, and field sanitation.
2. Scout on the same weekday. Look at a set number of plants, not only the damaged ones near the path.
3. Identify the pest and note whether natural enemies are present.
4. Act when damage or pest numbers cross a level you have agreed with extension guidance, not when you first see a single insect.
5. Choose the least disruptive allowed method and check afterwards whether it worked.
6. Write down the date, the pest, the action, and the result.

Required materials:
- Scouting plan
- Identification help from extension
- Allowed inputs
- A notebook

Practical examples:
- Rice: perching sticks and water management before any spray against leaf feeders
- Cotton: weekly square inspection and a trap crop before a neem or Bt application

Related crops: Rice, Cotton, Tomato, Mango

Precautions:
- IPM is not a product you can buy. A bottle labelled IPM is still just one tool.
- Thresholds from another region may not match your variety and climate.
- An organic farm cannot adopt the chemical step of a conventional IPM leaflet.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

Title: Jeevamrutham
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Jeevamrutham, also spelled jeevamrutha, is a fermented preparation associated with Subhash Palekar's Natural Farming. A commonly published farm recipe uses cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, a handful of uncontaminated living soil, and water, fermented for a few days in the shade and stirred. Farmers apply the liquid to soil to encourage microbial activity. Published recipes differ, and the preparation is not a measured chemical fertilizer. It works best where the soil already has organic matter and crop residue for microbes to feed on.

Benefits:
- Low-cost inoculant made on the farm
- Encourages biological activity when soil has residue to feed on
- Uses materials available on a mixed farm
- Can be applied through irrigation water

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Mix about 10 kilograms of fresh cow dung, 5 to 10 litres of cow urine, 1 to 2 kilograms of jaggery, 1 to 2 kilograms of pulse flour, and a handful of soil from an untreated field into about 200 litres of water. Recipes vary; treat these figures as a widely circulated traditional proportion, not a unique standard.
2. Keep the drum in the shade. Stir clockwise and anticlockwise morning and evening.
3. Use it after about two to five days, when it has fermented but before it smells putrid. Do not store it for weeks.
4. Apply to moist soil, often along rows or with irrigation, at a traditional rate of about 200 litres per acre. Increase organic residues if the soil is bare and sandy.

Required materials:
- Cow dung
- Cow urine
- Jaggery
- Pulse flour
- Living soil
- A 200-litre drum and clean water

Practical examples:
- Soil drench for millets, cotton, and vegetables in natural-farming systems
- Used alongside mulch, not instead of all organic matter

Related crops: Cotton, Tomato, Chilli, Groundnut

Precautions:
- Use dung and urine only from animals that fit your certification rule.
- Do not drink the preparation.
- It does not replace bulky manure on a nutrient-hungry crop such as banana or sugarcane.
- If the drum smells rotten rather than fermented, discard it away from drinking water.

Title: Natural pest management
Category: pest
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Natural pest management begins before any spray is mixed. A vigorous plant on well-drained soil, a crop rotation, clean seed, and a field that still harbours spiders, wasps, and ladybirds will need fewer interventions. The farmer scouts on a fixed walk each week, identifies the actual insect, and acts when damage is rising rather than on a calendar. Botanical sprays such as neem oil are tools with limits. They can also harm beneficial insects and pollinators if they are used carelessly.

Benefits:
- Reduces dependence on synthetic pesticides.
- Protects pollinators and natural enemies when interventions are selective.
- Lowers the chance of residue problems at harvest.
- Builds farm knowledge through regular scouting records.

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Prevent: use healthy seed, resistant varieties, rotation, balanced organic matter, and sanitation.
2. Monitor: inspect leaves, stems, and the soil line. Record the pest, the crop stage, and the number of damaged plants.
3. Identify natural enemies already present before you spray.
4. Use physical and cultural methods first: hand removal, traps, mulch, trap crops, and destruction of badly infested residues.
5. If a spray is justified, choose a product allowed by your organic standard, mix the recommended dilution, and spray in the evening.
6. Return after a few days to see whether the treatment worked. Change the method if it did not, rather than simply spraying more often.

Required materials:
- Hand lens or close observation
- Field notebook
- Yellow sticky traps
- Neem oil and mild soap where a spray is appropriate
- Gloves and eye protection

Practical examples:
- Tomato fruit borer is reduced by picking damaged fruit every two days and by a marigold border, with neem used only if new damage continues.
- Rice leaf folders are watched first. A spray is considered only if damaged leaves increase across the field, not because a neighbour sprayed.

Related crops: Rice, Tomato, Cotton, Mango

Precautions:
- Botanical pesticides are still pesticides. Keep them off skin, eyes, fish ponds, and open flowers.
- Spraying at noon in hot weather can scorch leaves.
- Misidentifying a beneficial insect as a pest leads to unnecessary sprays.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

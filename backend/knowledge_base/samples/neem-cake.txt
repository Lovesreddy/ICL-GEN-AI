Title: Neem cake
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Neem cake is the solid left after oil is pressed from neem seed. It contains plant nutrients and azadirachtin-related compounds that can discourage some soil insects and nematodes when mixed into soil. It is an amendment, not a stand-alone fertilizer. Quality varies with how the oil was extracted. Use cake that is free of adulterants and allowed by your organic standard.

Benefits:
- Adds organic nitrogen slowly
- Traditional soil amendment against some soil pests
- Useful in vegetable beds and spice crops
- Stores well when kept dry

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Buy cake from a known mill and keep it dry.
2. Crush lumps and mix the cake into the soil or the planting row at land preparation. A commonly cited educational rate is about 200 kilograms per acre, adjusted for crop and soil.
3. Do not place a thick dry layer in direct contact with germinating seed.
4. Combine it with compost. Cake alone does not rebuild soil structure.

Required materials:
- Neem cake
- Compost
- Gloves

Practical examples:
- Mixed into turmeric beds
- A small amount in tomato planting holes together with compost

Related crops: Turmeric, Tomato, Chilli, Banana

Precautions:
- Dust irritates eyes and lungs. Wear a cloth and gloves.
- Do not dump surplus cake into ponds; neem compounds can affect aquatic life.
- Adulterated cake may contain materials forbidden in organic systems.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

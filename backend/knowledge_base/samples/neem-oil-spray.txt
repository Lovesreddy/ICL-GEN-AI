Title: Neem oil spray
Category: pest
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Neem oil, pressed from neem seed, is used as a contact and antifeedant spray. A commonly taught educational dilution for field crops is about 5 millilitres of neem oil per litre of water, plus a few drops of a mild liquid soap so the oil mixes. Some products are already formulated and must be diluted exactly as their label says. Neem is most useful on aphids, whiteflies, and small caterpillars. It will not rescue a field of fully grown borers inside fruit.

Benefits:
- Widely available botanical option
- Useful against several sucking pests
- Breaks down in sunlight, so timing matters
- Can be part of an organic input list when the product is approved

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Confirm the product is allowed by your certifier if the crop is certified.
2. Mix about 5 millilitres of pure neem oil and about 1 millilitre of mild soap in a small volume of water, then make up to 1 litre. Shake often. If you use a commercial formulation, ignore this household dilution and follow its label.
3. Test the spray on a few leaves and wait a day, especially on tender seedlings and in hot weather.
4. Spray in the evening, covering the underside of leaves. Keep people, animals, and bees out of the drift.
5. Repeat only if monitoring shows the pest is still increasing. More frequent spraying is not automatically better.

Required materials:
- Neem oil or a labelled neem formulation
- Mild soap as an emulsifier if required
- Clean water
- A sprayer
- Gloves and eye protection

Practical examples:
- Aphids on chilli
- Whiteflies on tomato
- Early leaf-feeding caterpillars on vegetables

Related crops: Tomato, Chilli, Cotton, Wheat

Precautions:
- Oil and soap can burn leaves in strong sun.
- Neem is toxic to some aquatic organisms. Do not wash the sprayer into a pond.
- Protect eyes. Concentrated oil irritates skin.
- Do not spray open blossoms while pollinators are foraging.

Title: Panchagavya
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Panchagavya is a traditional preparation built from cow dung, cow urine, milk, curd, and ghee, often with jaggery, banana, and tender coconut water in farm recipes. It is fermented for about two to three weeks and then diluted before spraying. Farmers use it as a growth promoter. Evidence and recipes vary. It should be filtered and diluted, and it should not be sprayed on open flowers when bees are working.

Benefits:
- Uses on-farm ingredients
- Applied as a dilute foliar spray
- Some farmers report greener growth when the crop is otherwise well fed
- Can be prepared in a covered clay or plastic drum

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Follow a recipe from your extension service or certifier. A widely circulated version ferments dung and ghee first, then adds urine, milk, curd, and other ingredients over several days.
2. Stir daily and keep the vessel shaded and covered with a cloth so gases can escape.
3. After fermentation, filter the liquid. A commonly used spray dilution is about 3 percent, or 30 millilitres per litre of water. Confirm the dilution you were taught.
4. Spray in the evening, on a healthy crop, and wash equipment afterwards.

Required materials:
- Cow dung, urine, milk, curd, and ghee
- Jaggery and fruit if the chosen recipe includes them
- A fermenting vessel
- A sprayer and clean water

Practical examples:
- Monthly foliar spray on vegetables during active growth
- Used on some fruit crops before flowering, stopped while bees are dense in the blossom

Related crops: Tomato, Mango, Banana, Chilli

Precautions:
- The liquid has a strong smell and can spoil if stored carelessly.
- Test a few plants before spraying a whole field in hot weather.
- Certified organic farms must check that every ingredient is allowed.
- Keep the concentrate away from children and drinking-water pots.

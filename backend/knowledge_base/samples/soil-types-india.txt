Title: Major soil groups in India
Category: soil
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

The types of soils in India are commonly described using eight broad groups. Alluvial soils occur across the northern plains and river deltas; their texture and fertility vary, and rice, wheat, sugarcane, or jute may suit different locations. Black (regur) soils are clay-rich, hold water, and swell when wet and crack as they dry; cotton, sorghum, soybean, and pulses are grown in suitable areas. Red and yellow soils occur widely across the eastern and southern plateau; many are relatively low in organic matter, so millets, pulses, or groundnut depend on rainfall and soil management. Laterite soils form in strongly leached, high-rainfall regions; tea, coffee, and cashew are grown in some areas with suitable management. Arid soils are common in dry western regions and are often sandy with low organic matter; millets and pulses depend on local rainfall and available water. Forest and mountain soils vary with slope and elevation; horticulture and tea are grown in suitable locations, while steep or shallow soils need erosion protection. Saline and alkaline soils contain excess soluble salts or sodium that can restrict crops; testing and drainage assessment are needed before choosing reclamation steps. Peaty and marshy soils have high organic matter and often remain wet; rice and other wetland crops are grown where local water conditions allow. These are broad regional groups, not a diagnosis of an individual field.

Benefits:
- Broad soil groups help compare landscapes and plan what to test.
- Understanding texture, drainage, and salinity helps avoid crop choices that do not fit field conditions.

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Use the broad group as a starting clue, not as a substitute for observing the field.
2. Check texture, drainage, pH, salinity, and organic matter through an appropriate soil test.
3. Choose crops using the field result together with rainfall, irrigation water, season, and local extension advice.

Required materials:
- Field history and location notes
- Representative soil sample
- Soil laboratory report when available

Practical examples:
- A black clay soil may hold water well but require careful drainage and timing of field operations.
- A sandy arid soil may need water-conserving management, but irrigation water quality should also be checked.

Related crops: Rice, Wheat, Cotton, Groundnut, Tea

Precautions:
- Do not identify a field solely by soil color or a regional map.
- Do not apply gypsum, lime, or other amendments for salinity or sodicity without a soil test and local advice.
- Crop suitability varies within each group; check field conditions and local recommendations.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

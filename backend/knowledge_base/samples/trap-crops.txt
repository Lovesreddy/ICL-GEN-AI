Title: Trap crops
Category: pest
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

A trap crop is more attractive to a pest than the crop you want to protect. Pests gather on the trap and can be removed by cutting, crushing, or a targeted spray, while the main crop is left alone. The trap must be planted early enough to be attractive at the right time, and it must be destroyed before the pests multiply and move back. An unmanaged trap crop becomes a nursery.

Benefits:
- Concentrates pests in a small area
- Reduces spraying of the main crop
- Makes scouting faster
- Can be combined with biological control

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Learn which local trap works for your pest. Examples used in extension programmes include marigold or a designated border for some cotton and vegetable pests, and mustard as a trap for some pests of cabbage-family crops.
2. Sow the trap earlier or at the spacing that makes it more attractive than the main crop.
3. Inspect the trap twice a week.
4. Destroy the infested trap plants before the next generation of the pest emerges. Removing them late is worse than not planting them.

Required materials:
- Trap-crop seed
- Labour for timely destruction
- A scouting routine

Practical examples:
- A marigold or okra border watched for bollworm eggs beside cotton
- Mustard rows in a cabbage-family garden, pulled and buried when they are heavily infested

Related crops: Cotton, Tomato, Chilli

Precautions:
- A forgotten trap crop increases the pest.
- Do not put the trap on the downwind side only and then ignore the rest of the border.
- Confirm the local pest-trap pairing with extension staff. Attractiveness is regional.

This is educational guidance drawn from widely described organic farming practice. Rates, varieties, and legal rules vary by soil, season, and country. Confirm important decisions with a local agriculture extension officer or your organic certifier.

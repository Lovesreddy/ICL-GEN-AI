Title: Vermicompost
Category: fertilizer
Source: Organic Farming Guide educational knowledge base

Vermicompost is the stable material harvested from a worm bed. It is darker and finer than ordinary compost and is usually applied in smaller quantities because it is more concentrated and more work to produce. It improves seedling mixes and planting holes. It does not, by itself, meet the nutrient removal of a high-yielding sugarcane or rice crop.

Benefits:
- Fine texture for nurseries
- Steady nutrient release
- Improves soil aggregation
- Useful side-dressing for vegetables

Step-by-step instructions:
1. Produce castings as described in the vermicomposting guide, and cure them in the shade until they no longer heat.
2. For vegetables, mix a modest amount into the bed or planting hole along with a larger basal dressing of compost.
3. For nurseries, blend castings with soil and coarse compost rather than sowing into pure castings.
4. Side-dress around established plants and cover with mulch so the material stays moist and is not blown away.

Required materials:
- Finished vermicompost
- Basal compost
- Mulch

Practical examples:
- Tomato and chilli planting holes
- Potting mix for mango and other tree nurseries

Related crops: Tomato, Chilli, Turmeric

Precautions:
- Do not use unfinished, heating material against seed.
- Store castings in the shade with some air, not in a sealed wet sack.
